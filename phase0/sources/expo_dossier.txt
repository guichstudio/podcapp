P R O J E C T  S TAT E M E N T
I went to China intending to photograph a territory.
I ended up photographing what exists between people.
Over three months, from Wudang to Hunchun,
through Chongqing, Xi'an and more rural parts of
the country, my attention gradually shifted from the
territory itself to the human relationships unfolding
within it.
I spent the first two months in a traditional kung fu
school in the Wudang Mountains. There,
knowledge was passed on through repetition: a
gesture observed, learned, then repeated until it
became instinctive. Around the school, temples,
mountains and local residents placed this idea of
transmission within a sense of time far larger than
my own.
I was not looking for spectacle. What interested me
was often happening just beside it: a meal, a
pause, a body at rest, a conversation, an open
door, people gathered around a table.
This attention to the moments in between guided
the rest of the journey. In the places I passed
through, I was struck by the way private life
seemed to spill continuously into shared space:
eating, playing, cooking, working, dancing, waiting
together. At times, the street became an extension
of the home.
I am not trying to explain China or to offer a
definitive portrait of it. I remain an outsider to the
places I move through, and that distance is part of
the work. I photograph exclusively on 35mm film,
without retouching. I cannot immediately verify the
image or reconstruct afterward what I saw. That
uncertainty leaves room for accident, doubt and
memory.
In Hunchun, where Chinase territory narrows
between Russia and North Korea, the journey ends
in front of the lines that divide space, after weeks
spent observing what allows people to share it.
The Space Between Us is therefore about China,
but also about a more universal question: how
ordinary gestures, shared spaces and acts of
transmission allow people to build a life together.

C U R A T O R I A L  S T R U C T U R E
Five movements, rather than five destinations.
0 1  /  W U D A N G TRANSMIT
The body, repetition, master and student. Tradition appears through everyday gestures rather than
through folklore.
0 2  /  C H O N G Q I N G COEXIST
Within urban density, the work observes how collective life persists, reorganizes itself and occupies
every available interstice.
0 3  /  H O N G  K O N G BELONG
Hong Kong introduces another form of belonging - shaped by density, movement and a constantly
shifting sense of identity. Here, community is less about rootedness than about finding one's place
within a city in permanent transition.
0 4  /  R U R A L  C H I N ASHARE
The street as an extension of the home: meals, conversations, games and idle moments create a
visible social space.
0 5  /  H U N C H U N BOUNDARIES
The border becomes landscape. The project closes on a tension between what brings people
together and what divides territories.

E X H I B I T I O N  C O N C E P T
A journey that begins with territory and ends with
connection.
The exhibition is conceived as a progression rather
than a travel itinerary. Places act as anchors, but
the journey is structured by ideas of transmission,
coexistence, memory, sharing and borders.
The opening images can give more space to
landscape and context. As the exhibition unfolds,
bodies, gestures and relationships become
increasingly present, until collective life emerges as
the true subject.
Hunchun acts as a final counterpoint. After
observing the ways people share space, the border
abruptly reintroduces the question of how space is
divided.
The hanging should preserve space and rhythm.
The photographs are not conceived as
documentary evidence accompanied by long
explanations, but as fragments capable of
speaking to one another.
Variations in scale can distinguish structural
images from more intimate ones. Text remains
sparse: an introductory statement, the five
movements, and minimal captions.
Viewer experience:
to enter expecting a journey through China, and
leave questioning the gestures, spaces and
relationships through which people create a shared
life.
The proposal can be adapted to the space, number of prints and curatorial context of the gallery or institution.

M AT E R I A L I T Y  &  P R I N T  P R O P O S A L
The image as trace, not reconstruction.
Film is not only an aesthetic choice: it creates a distance
between lived experience and the resulting image.
PROCESS Exclusively photographed on 35mm film.
IMAGE Photographs are not digitally retouched, preserving the integrity of the original
negative and the accidents of the moment.
PRINTS Proposal: fine-art pigment or traditional photographic prints, to be defined with
the gallery according to the body of work and the exhibition space.
FORMATS Two principal scales are recommended: intimate formats for gestures and
portraits; larger formats for landscapes and collective scenes.
EDITIONS Limited editions to be defined before release, with certificates and consistent
numbering by format.
HANGING Restrained framing and generous spacing. The photographs are intended to
relate through rhythm, gaze, distance and repetition rather than strict
chronology.
Dimensions, papers, framing and editions are intentionally left open for discussion in order to build a proposal that
responds coherently to the exhibition space.

P H O T O G R A P H I C  P R A C T I C E
Between reality and perception.
My photographic practice developed in response to several years spent constructing images. In
Paris, I worked across audiovisual production, film, fashion and visual staging for music, before
working in New York and San Francisco on storytelling and identity-building for technology
companies.
That background taught me to think of an image as a tool capable of constructing narrative, identity
and perception. Photography gradually became the reverse movement: leaving the controlled
image behind in order to return to something more instinctive, uncertain and immediate.
I work exclusively on 35mm film and without retouching. This constraint preserves an element of
accident and prevents me from reshaping an encounter after the fact. I am less interested in the
documentary precision of a place than in what it leaves behind: a sensation, a memory, a trace.
For me, a photograph is therefore not an answer. It is a space left open to the viewer.
“A photograph becomes less a proof of reality
than a trace of our passage through it.”

B I O G R A P H Y
Louis Guichard
Louis Guichard, born in 1997, is a French
photographer and visual artist based between
Europe and the United States.
His practice grew out of a broader background in
image-making. In Paris, he spent five years
working across audiovisual production, film, fashion
and visual staging for music before moving to New
York and developing storytelling and branding
projects for technology companies between New
York and San Francisco.
Alongside this work, travel across the United
States, China and India gradually made
photography his primary medium. In contrast to the
constructed images of his commercial practice, he
looks to film photography for something more
immediate and uncertain.
He photographs exclusively on 35mm film, without
retouching, developing a body of work around
memory, perception and the traces left by places
and encounters.
